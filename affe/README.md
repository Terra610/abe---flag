# AFFE — Applied Fiscal & Financial Exposure

## Function

AFFE quantifies the financial impact of divergence identified in CDA and CFF.

Divergence produces measurable economic cost.

AFFE converts divergence conditions into financial exposure.

---

## Input

- CDA output (divergence classification)
- CFF output (funding alignment and misuse)
- Cost variables (enforcement, incarceration, administrative, economic suppression)
- Sector-specific financial data

---

## Output

- Total financial exposure
- Cost per divergence condition
- Aggregated economic loss
- Sector-level financial impact

---

## Calculation Logic

AFFE calculates:

- Direct costs (enforcement, detention, court processing)
- Indirect costs (lost income, reduced mobility, suppressed economic activity)
- Systemic costs (market drag, reduced participation, credit degradation)

Each divergence condition contributes to total financial exposure.

---

## Deterministic Rule

Divergence produces cost.

If a condition is classified as divergence:
→ it contributes to measurable financial exposure

No divergence condition is excluded.

---

## System Position

Upstream:  
- CDA (divergence classification)  
- CFF (funding alignment)

Downstream:  
- CIRI (impact and recovery modeling)  
- CIBS (system behavior and feedback)

---

## System Role

AFFE translates divergence into quantifiable economic terms.

It establishes the financial magnitude of misalignment across all evaluated conditions.

---

## Output Dependency

- CIRI uses AFFE output to model recovery potential and social impact
- CIBS uses AFFE output to evaluate systemic behavior and feedback loops
- Macro aggregates total exposure into economy-wide impact

---

## Core Principle

Divergence is not abstract.

Divergence produces measurable financial loss.

All financial exposure is traceable to divergence conditions.

---

## Result

AFFE produces a deterministic financial measurement of divergence.

This output defines the economic scale of misalignment within A.B.E.
